# Flood zones vs. reality: "not in a flood zone" does not mean safe
source: https://communityimpact.com/news/2018/06/08/hurricane-harvey-damage-report-harris-county
source: https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/18/1073/2018/nhess-18-1073-2018.pdf
source: https://maapnext.harriscountytx.gov/Understand-Your-Risk
source: https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/10-years-after-tax-day-flood-rice-researchers-warn-hidden-risk
source: https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/04/21/new-maps-reveal-hidden-flood-danger-across-houston/

- In Harvey, "a little less than half" of flooded Harris County homes were outside both the 100- and 500-year floodplains (county figure). One TV outlet reported about 75%, likely counting only the 100-year zone; use the county figure. (Community Impact)
- "The majority of fatalities were recovered outside the designated 100- and 500-year flood hazard areas." Only 22% of Harris County deaths were inside the 100-year zone. (NHESS 2018)
- "More than 25% of all flood insurance claims come from properties located in moderate- or low-risk areas." (Harris County MAAPnext)
- Rice researchers call "not in a flood zone" "a dangerous misnomer": "The lines on a FEMA map are often actuarial, not physical." (Rice, 2026)
- Where a storm tracks matters. In Rice's Tax Day re-modeling (as reported by KPRC), shifting the storm a few miles changed flooded homes over Clear Creek from about 3,600 to more than 13,000. (KPRC, Apr 2026)
- Flooding also comes from overloaded storm drains and roadside ditches, and sheet flow can reach homes not near any creek or bayou. (MAAPnext; https://harriscountyfws.org/Help)

What to do: treat a zone result as one input. Check ground elevation, ask neighbors about past flooding, and consider flood insurance regardless of zone.
