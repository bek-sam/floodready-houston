# Relying on power for a medical device (oxygen, ventilator, dialysis, refrigerated medicine)

- HHS emPOWER publishes ZIP-level counts of Medicare beneficiaries who rely on electricity-dependent equipment. It counts Medicare beneficiaries only, by ZIP, not individuals' locations. Map: https://empowerprogram.hhs.gov/empowermap (HHS emPOWER)
- Texas PUC Critical Care / Chronic Condition residential designation (PUC Substantive Rule 25.497): a physician certifies the condition and you apply through your electric provider using the PUC form. (ERCOT-hosted PUC form)
- The designation does not guarantee uninterrupted power or faster restoration. Keep your own backup plan. (KHOU/CenterPoint reporting)
- Keep a charged backup battery or other backup power for at least 3 days, and ask your device supplier for its outage instructions. (general preparedness, not official guidance)
- Register with STEAR (State of Texas Emergency Assistance Registry) or call 2-1-1. STEAR does not guarantee assistance. (TDEM)
- Dialysis patients: keep your clinic's backup plan and phone numbers and register with water and power companies. National Kidney Foundation 1-855-653-2273; KCER 1-866-901-3773. (CDC)
- If you use a generator, run it outdoors more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents and use a battery CO alarm (see power-outage-and-generators.md). (CDC)

What to do: talk to your doctor now about the PUC form, apply with your electric provider, charge backup power before a storm, and register with STEAR or 2-1-1. If someone's device fails or they feel dizzy, short of breath or confused, call 911.
