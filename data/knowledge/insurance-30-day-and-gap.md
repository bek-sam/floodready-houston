# Flood insurance: the 30-day wait and the coverage gap
source: https://www.floodsmart.gov/get-insured/buy-a-policy
source: https://communityimpact.com/news/2018/06/08/hurricane-harvey-damage-report-harris-county
source: https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/04/21/new-maps-reveal-hidden-flood-danger-across-houston/
source: https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/10-years-after-tax-day-flood-rice-researchers-warn-hidden-risk

- "Your flood insurance coverage will go into effect 30 days after your date of purchase." A policy bought when a storm is already forecast is too late. (FloodSmart.gov)
- Only 36% of Harris County homes that flooded in Harvey were covered by flood insurance. (Community Impact)
- Harris County had 295,108 NFIP policies in June 2022, and 91% of single-family policyholders faced premium increases under Risk Rating 2.0; as prices rise, participation tends to fall. (CSFI white paper: https://uphelp.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CSFI-White-Paper-An-Evaluation-of-Risk-Rating-2.0-Impacts-on-National-Flood-Insurance-Program-Affordability.pdf)
- A Rice researcher said in a spoken quote that "92% of at-risk homes do not have flood insurance coverage" (KPRC, Apr 2026). This is a quote, not a published statistic. Rice's release says "the vast majority would likely not have flood insurance."

What to do: buy well before the season peaks. Use the FloodReady insurance countdown to see whether a policy bought today is active before the end of hurricane season.
