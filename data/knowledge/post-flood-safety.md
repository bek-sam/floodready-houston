# After the flood: safety hazards
source: https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/18/1073/2018/nhess-18-1073-2018.pdf
source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Imelda_(2019)
source: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/07/08/npr-hurricane-beryl-texas

- Electrocution caused 4 Harvey deaths and one death in Imelda. Infection is another post-flood hazard. (NHESS 2018; Wikipedia, Imelda)
- 81% of Harvey deaths were drownings. Flood water can stay deep and fast after rain stops. (NHESS 2018)
- During Beryl a tree fell on a high-water rescue vehicle; rescue crews face hazards too. (MPR News)
- During Beryl about 2.2 million customers lost power, and half of Harris County storm deaths were heat-related. (MPR News; https://www.texastribune.org/2024/07/22/texas-hurricane-beryl-heat-deaths/)

What to do: stay out of flood water and away from downed power lines or standing water near electrical equipment. Do not drive through water. Plan for heat and power loss. Call 911 for emergencies. This card is general awareness drawn from the sources above, not official guidance.

## Mold
source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9198619/

- Houston Health Department focus groups with 31 residents found that cost led to long exposure to mold, and cleanup often did not follow guidance. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9198619/
- The Houston-3H study of 347 residents linked reported mold exposure to allergic symptoms lasting up to a year. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9198619/

What to do: dry out and clean flooded areas promptly, and seek help early if cost is a barrier rather than living with mold. See a doctor for lasting symptoms. Call 911 for emergencies. Not official guidance.
