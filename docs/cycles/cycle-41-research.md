## G-new2 Flooded cars (Recover)
Verified (WebFetch): none from NICB (403). SEARCH-ONLY: NICB describes VINCheck as a "free lookup service provided to the public" (https://nicb.org/node/79357; 2008 expansion added flood total losses). NICB Aug 2026 release (https://www.nicb.org/node/125014) advises VINCheck + history report + technician inspection; lists musty odor, water stains, rust, silt/mud. A clean result does not rule out flood damage (title washing; covers only participating insurers).
Card (Recover, "Buying or selling a used car"):
1. Run the VIN at NICB VINCheck (nicb.org/vincheck, free). It only lists insurer-reported total losses.
2. Clean result is not proof: look for musty smell, silt or rust under seats and in the trunk, water lines.
3. Get a vehicle history report and a mechanic inspection before paying.
4. Your own flooded car: do not start it; call your insurer.

## G-new4 FEMA denial / appeal (Recover)
Verified (WebFetch https://www.fema.gov/fact-sheet/how-appeal-fema-decision-4):
- "within 60 days of the date of the decision letter."
- "You must include your FEMA application number and disaster number on every page."
- Documents: receipts, bills, repair estimates, titles/deeds; letter lists which apply.
- Submit: DisasterAssistance.gov Upload Center; mail P.O. Box 10055, Hyattsville, MD 20782-8055; fax (800) 827-8112; Helpline 800-621-3362.
Card: 1. Read the letter for the reason (often missing documents). 2. Appeal within 60 days of the letter date, signed, with application and disaster number on every page. 3. Attach evidence (receipts, estimates, proof of ownership). 4. Upload at DisasterAssistance.gov or mail/fax; call 800-621-3362. Note: this fact sheet is a prior-disaster edition; the deadline matches the existing card, but tell users to use their own letter's address/deadline. Update knowledge card from "confirm deadline" to 60 days with this source.

## G-new5 Floodwater contamination
Coverage: p39.ts has only the title "Electrical and floodwater safety" (grep found no contamination, wound or sewage strings), so floodwater health is NOT covered. Add.
Verified (WebFetch https://www.cdc.gov/floods/safety/index.html): "Flood water may contain trash."; know your last tetanus shot if a wound "becomes contaminated"; wear rubber boots and plastic gloves; discard drywall, rugs, cloth that cannot be disinfected; disinfect with "1 cup of bleach and 1 gallon of water"; wash hands with soap; follow local officials on drinking water.
Card: avoid wading; cover cuts, boots and gloves; wash hands; throw out porous soaked items; boil/use water only when officials say safe; check tetanus date. Drop: Texas A&M "125x E. coli" stays CBS-sourced only.
